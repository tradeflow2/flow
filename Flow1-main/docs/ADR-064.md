# ADR-064: Commercial Snapshot, Discount Approval and Pre-Invoice Integrity

**Status:** Proposed
**Date:** 2026-11-15
**Scope:** Stage 5 – MVP
**Supersedes:** Only the conflicting provisions of ADR-013 and ADR-060 within the Stage 5 scope.

## Context

Stage 5 introduces configurable discount policies, request-level discounts, versioned commercial snapshots, discount approval, and Pre-Invoice integrity controls.

Existing decisions in ADR-013 and ADR-060 defer or exclude parts of approval functionality from the MVP. These provisions conflict with the approved Stage 5 contract.

This ADR defines the Stage 5 implementation boundary while preserving all non-conflicting decisions in earlier ADRs.

## Decision

### 1. Discount Policy and Authorization

- Discount policies are configurable per organization and applicable role/scope.
- Policy evaluation uses the role of `discount_applied_by`, not `purchase_requests.created_by`.
- The policy version and applicable parameters must be captured in the Commercial Snapshot.
- Direct discount authority and maximum requestable discount are separate policy limits.
- No discount limits or role-specific amounts shall be hard-coded.
- If no valid policy exists, non-zero discounts must fail closed.
- A zero discount does not require approval.

### 2. Approval Authority

- In the Stage 5 MVP, only the `ADMIN` role may approve a discount.
- The authorization check must be centralized.
- The MVP does not introduce a separate `SALES_MANAGER` role.
- Approval authority is distinct from the authority to apply a discount.

### 3. Maker-Checker and Self-Approval

Self-approval is prohibited.

An approver must not be the same user as any of the following:

- The user who created the purchase request.
- The user who applied the discount.
- The user who requested the approval.

This restriction applies even when the approver has the `ADMIN` role.

A self-approval attempt must return PostgreSQL error code `42501` and must not change the approval state.

### 4. Commercial Snapshot

- A Commercial Snapshot is an independent, versioned record of the commercial terms of a purchase request.
- Each approval must refer to one specific snapshot version.
- The snapshot must capture all commercially relevant information, including:
  - Customer or trading party information.
  - Product and item identity.
  - Quantity and unit.
  - Unit price.
  - Item-level discount.
  - Request-level discount and its allocation.
  - Item-level tax amount.
  - Addition or removal of items.
  - Other commercial fields that affect the transaction.
- Non-commercial notes must not increment the commercial version.
- A change to any commercially relevant field invalidates the previous approval and requires a new snapshot.
- A change to a discount policy alone does not invalidate an existing approval. The policy version and parameters used must remain recorded in the snapshot.

### 5. Approval Lifecycle

- Approval is performed while the purchase request is in `DRAFT`.
- An approval is associated with one specific Commercial Snapshot.
- A rejected approval leaves the purchase request in `DRAFT`.
- A rejected request may be commercially edited and submitted for a new approval.
- A previous approval cannot authorize a newer snapshot.
- Manual cancellation of an `APPROVED` approval is not permitted.
- A commercial change invalidates the previous approval through the `INVALIDATED` state.
- Approval has no independent time-based expiration. Its validity is tied to the snapshot version.

### 6. Submission and Issue Gate

- A purchase request may move from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` without discount approval.
- Discount approval is not a prerequisite for submission.
- If a discount requires approval, the Pre-Invoice cannot be issued until the required approval is valid.
- The integrity gate applies when issuing a Pre-Invoice through the `DRAFT → ISSUED` transition.
- Creating a Pre-Invoice in `DRAFT` does not itself require discount approval.

### 7. Pre-Invoice Integrity

A Pre-Invoice must be generated from the exact valid Commercial Snapshot associated with the purchase request.

The system must not use live purchase request data as a substitute for the approved snapshot.

At issue time, the system must validate, within the same transaction:

- The applicable Commercial Snapshot.
- The snapshot version.
- Commercial amounts and item-level values.
- The required discount approval, where applicable.
- The relationship between the Pre-Invoice and its source snapshot.

If the snapshot or approval is stale, invalid, or inconsistent, issuance must fail.

### 8. Purchase Request Totals

Existing `purchase_requests` total fields retain their current semantics and database constraints.

The Commercial Snapshot is the authoritative commercial and financial source for Stage 5 Pre-Invoice generation and related financial documents.

Existing totals must not be reinterpreted as final post-discount commercial amounts unless their existing semantics explicitly support that interpretation.

### 9. Discount Allocation

Request-level discounts are allocated proportionally across eligible items using the Largest Remainder Method.

Tie-breaking order:

1. `line_no`
2. `id`

The allocation must ensure:

- The sum of allocated discounts equals the request-level discount exactly.
- No item receives an allocation exceeding its eligible amount.
- Items with zero eligible net value receive zero allocation.

### 10. Tax Treatment

- Tax is calculated after item-level discounts and allocated request-level discounts.
- Tax amounts are stored at item level in `commercial_snapshot_items.tax_amount`.
- Item-level tax amounts must be consistent with the post-discount taxable base supplied by the authorized business process.
- The sum of item tax amounts must equal the snapshot's total tax amount.
- Stage 5 does not introduce a complete tax engine, tax-rate management system, exemption engine, or jurisdiction-specific tax rules.

### 11. Error Handling

- Self-approval attempts use PostgreSQL error code `42501`.
- Invalid or stale state transitions must use the appropriate state-conflict error, subject to compatibility with the existing database error mapping.
- Error mapping must not silently convert authorization failures into successful operations.

### 12. Database and Migration Constraints

- Existing protected migrations `001`, `002`, `004`, `005`, and `006` must not be modified.
- `runner.py` must not be modified for Stage 5 unless a separately approved change explicitly requires it.
- Stage 5 database changes must be introduced through the next approved migration.
- Required migration registration, capability declarations, and test-runner ordering must be maintained.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Discount authority is configurable rather than hard-coded.
- Approval is tied to an immutable commercial version.
- Self-approval is prevented.
- Rejected requests remain editable.
- Submission is not unnecessarily blocked by approval.
- Pre-Invoice issuance validates the exact commercial state and required approval.
- Existing database behavior and protected migrations remain intact.

### Trade-offs

- Commercial changes require a new snapshot and, where applicable, a new approval.
- Approval history must be retained to provide an auditable record.
- Pre-Invoice issuance requires transactional integrity checks.
- Stage 5 does not provide a complete tax engine or a general document-signature workflow.

## Supersession

This ADR supersedes only the provisions that conflict with the Stage 5 decisions in:

- **ADR-013:** the phase and scope of maker-checker/approval functionality, to the extent that Stage 5 introduces discount approval in the MVP.
- **ADR-060:** the deferral of discount approval, to the extent that Stage 5 introduces the defined discount approval workflow in the MVP.

All non-conflicting provisions of ADR-013 and ADR-060 remain valid.

This ADR does not introduce a general manager-signature workflow for sales documents. Such functionality remains a separate future capability.
